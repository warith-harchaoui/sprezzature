## Film global, `sprezzature`

**X, Bluesky, Mastodon**

> You can proofread a sentence by reading it back.
>
> You cannot do that with a chart.
>
> So Sprezzature renders the figure, looks at it, looks again with the colour
> gone, and only then ships. Ten skills for Claude Code and OpenCode.
>
> sprezzature.ai

**LinkedIn**

> A sentence, you can proofread. You read it back and the mistake is right
> there. A chart, you cannot. Whether it is right lives in the pixels, not in
> the code: a label slid off the edge, a legend sitting on the data, two series
> that collapse into one muddy colour for a reader who cannot tell them apart.
> None of that shows up when you read the source.
>
> So Sprezzature builds the looking into the loop. Render the figure to a plain
> image with a deterministic tool. Look at it. Look again in grayscale and
> through colour blind eyes you do not physically have. Fix the source. Render
> again. Ship only when there is nothing left to catch.
>
> The same discipline runs one level up. Before the suite trusts a local model,
> it seeds a known flaw into a text and a known defect into an image, and hires
> only the model that catches both.
>
> Ten Claude and OpenCode skills. Eight packages on PyPI, each reachable five
> ways: a command line, a Python library, an HTTP API, MCP tools, and the skill
> itself, all over one implementation. 960 tests. Nothing has to leave the
> machine.
>
> sprezzature.ai

## Accessibilité

> You cannot see your own chart the way a colour blind reader does. The
> information is not in your eye, and no amount of care changes that.
>
> So the simulator renders those other eyes and the loop looks through them.
> Twenty accessibility rules, WCAG contrast, colour vision simulation, alt text
> and captions. None of it leaves your machine.
>
> sprezzature.ai
