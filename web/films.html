    Eleven short films

    One on the suite, and one for each of its ten packages. Nothing in them is a mock-up: the figures are the shipped SVG files, the maps are drawn on a real projection, the form was generated from the command that is installed, and the colour vision simulations came out of sprezzature-colors.

    the whole suite

    Sprezzature 60 s

    Design is know-how, and most of it is already written down: thirty laws of UX, contrast ratios, a 2009 paper on colour vision, a projection from 2018. The suite is what happens when you point that know-how both ways — at making a thing, and at auditing it.

    What it shows: eight sources named, four real outputs, the Ralph Eyeball Loop running on a shipped figure, and what ten packages add up to.

    sprezzature-ui

    UI 60 s

    The smallest brick, on purpose. Its job is the aesthetic judgement written in prose — the three-Roboto rule, the design tokens, what an interface has to respect — plus the one part of that judgement a machine can carry out.

    What it shows: the best cost sentence in the suite, in two halves, and what the package ships: nine components, nine snippets, six font files.

    sprezzature-cli-gui

    CLI → GUI 60 s

    A tool nobody will type, wrapped in a page. It does not read the help text — it reads the parser object, through argparse, Click or Typer, and falls back to running --help only when there is no parser to reach.

    What it shows: two captures of the page it generates for make-figure, taken from the command that is installed rather than from a mock-up.

    sprezzature-publish

    Publish 60 s

    A document, becoming a site. Nine tools: a linter that runs on the standard library in milliseconds, favicons from one logo, the indexes a crawler and a language model both want, and readalong, which binds the narration to the page.

    What it shows: all nine named, then the boundary the package keeps — the voices live in sprezzature-audio and are called as a subprocess.

    sprezzature-accessibility

    Accessibility 60 s

    An unmuted autoplay masks a screen reader, and the markup says so on its own. Twenty rules from WCAG, each naming the element, the line and the rule it broke; six of them repair themselves, where there is exactly one safe repair.

    What it shows: three findings the source alone can prove, what --fix is allowed to touch, and the same rule pointed the other way, writing the CSS.

    sprezzature-colors

    Colors 60 s

    Light grey on white reads fine — to you, indoors. About one man in twelve sees red and green as the same bar, and no amount of care changes that, because the information is not in their eye.

    What it shows: all four simulations in turn, produced by the project's own simulate_cvd.py from the Machado matrices: protanopia, deuteranopia, tritanopia, grayscale.

    sprezzature-audio

    Audio 60 s

    One modality, read in both directions. Captions turn sound into timed text; narration turns text into timed sound, landing as a WAV plus a WebVTT — exactly like a transcript does, which is what lets a page follow the reading sentence by sentence.

    What it shows: the models named, the two files narration lands as, and what audit_captions measures about a track that is present but unreadable.

    sprezzature-ux-laws

    Laws of UX 60 s

    Nobody says “that violates Hick’s Law”. They say the page is overwhelming, or that they can never find the button. Thirty laws, curated by Jon YABLONSKI; eight of them a machine can decide from the markup alone.

    What it shows: the eight the auditor checks, three findings it can state exactly, and the boundary it does not cross — whether the screen answers the right question stays a human call.

    sprezzature-figures

    Figures 60 s

    167 kinds of chart across 52 categories, every one hand-authored. No charting library sits underneath any of it: each generator writes the SVG markup itself, and the vector is the deliverable rather than an export.

    What it shows: ten real figures from the gallery, the categories nobody else ships, and the Ralph Eyeball Loop with fourteen seconds to show its work.

    sprezzature-maps

    Maps 60 s

    A column of countries is a map waiting to happen. Real coastlines, a real projection, eight kilometres of relief — and a choice about which distortion you are willing to accept, stated rather than defaulted to.

    What it shows: six situation plates and nothing else — four reference, two areas-of-control — and the density map, which draws no coastline at all and still reads as land.

    sprezzature-video

    Video 60 s

    The brick that made the other ten films, and the one that checks them. A film is a claim with a soundtrack: every number on screen is verified against the code that produces it, including the numbers nobody declared.

    What it shows: the two kinds of claim, the three provenance classes an asset can have, and why captions are measured off the voice file rather than guessed.

    About these films

    • Each film exists twice, and the two do not mix: an English film is English throughout — on screen, in the voice, in the subtitles — and a French film is French throughout. This page serves the English cut; the French one is a click away.
    • The voices come from the suite itself: sprezzature-audio through Kokoro, with a pronunciation lexicon so that WebVTT, RTTM, OKLCH and GMTED2010 are said the same way in every film and in both languages. The same call writes the subtitles, off the same timeline as the audio.
    • Each film is cut three ways and all three are on this page: wide for a screen, tall for a phone held upright, square for a feed. Pick one above the player. The three are frame-synced, so the same sentence lands on the same second whichever you watch; only the layout moves. All of them are 4K.
    • They follow Guy KAWASAKI’s order, which is why the rail above each frame says where you are: the problem first, then what the package does about it, then the part you would not guess — and the sources it rests on, named so you can go and check them.
    • They were built with the same discipline they describe. Every scene was rendered to a still and looked at before the film was encoded, which is how the sixty-second set was caught quoting a catalogue size the package had outgrown months earlier — off a screenshot taken in September — naming a font the package does not ship, and counting twelve font files where there are six.
    • Two of those were typography rather than fact, and they now have a test instead of an eye: it measures every line of film text in the font the films are set in, and fails if one wraps and leaves its last word standing alone.
